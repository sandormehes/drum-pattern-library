= Source Register

This register separates listening references and historical context from the library's original notation. A reference attached to an original or traditional study supports its context; it does not imply that the grid is a note-for-note transcription.

== Jamaican foundations

[[src-bradley]]
Lloyd Bradley, _Bass Culture: When Reggae Was King_ (Viking, 2000):: Historical account of Jamaican popular music, sound-system culture, reggae, dub, and dancehall. Used for broad chronology and terminology.

[[src-veal]]
Michael E. Veal, _Dub: Soundscapes and Shattered Songs in Jamaican Reggae_ (Wesleyan University Press, 2007):: Study of dub's musical practices, studio methods, rhythmic space, and international legacy.

[[src-katz]]
David Katz, _Solid Foundation: An Oral History of Reggae_ (Bloomsbury, revised edition, 2012):: Artist- and producer-led oral history used to cross-check descriptions of reggae rhythm styles and the digital transition.

[[src-one-drop]]
Bob Marley & The Wailers, “One Drop,” _Survival_ (Island Records, 1979):: Listening reference for the named reggae feel. The library pattern is an educational reduction, not a transcription.

[[src-rockers]]
Augustus Pablo, “Rockers Rock,” single (Rockers International, 1975):: Listening reference for the rockers aesthetic and for variation beyond a single universal drum formula.

[[src-sleng-teng]]
Wayne Smith, “Under Me Sleng Teng,” single (Jammy's Records, 1985):: Landmark listening reference for Jamaica's digital dancehall transition and machine-built riddims.

== UK developments

[[src-krust]]
Robert McCallum, link:https://daily.redbullmusicacademy.com/2015/03/dj-krust-interview-2015[“Interview: Bristol's DJ Krust”], Red Bull Music Academy Daily (2015; accessed 2026-09-02):: First-person discussion with a jungle and drum & bass producer, used as context for breakbeat-led UK sound-system music.

[[src-dreem-teem]]
Bill Brewster and Frank Broughton, link:https://daily.redbullmusicacademy.com/2018/01/dreem-teem-interview[“Dreem Teem on the Birth of UK Garage”], Red Bull Music Academy Daily (interview recorded 2005, published 2018; accessed 2026-09-02):: Oral history of UK garage's emergence, pirate radio, and its relationship to grime and dubstep.

[[src-reynolds-ukg]]
Simon Reynolds, link:https://daily.redbullmusicacademy.com/2012/05/simon-reynolds-ukg-rb[“UKG x R&B”], Red Bull Music Academy Daily (2012; accessed 2026-09-02):: Overview and listening guide for late-1990s 2-step garage and its rhythmic, vocal, and pirate-radio context.

[[src-magnetic-man]]
Red Bull Music Academy, link:https://daily.redbullmusicacademy.com/2011/10/magnetic-man-interview[“Magnetic Man on the Early Days of UK Bass”] (2011; accessed 2026-09-02):: Interview with Artwork, Benga, and Skream covering the movement from garage toward dubstep and the role of half-step, grime, and Jamaican dancehall remixes.

[[src-plastician]]
Plastician, link:https://daily.redbullmusicacademy.com/2017/01/plastician-feature[“Plastician Talks Grime”], Red Bull Music Academy Daily (2017; accessed 2026-09-02):: First-person account of the overlapping early grime and dubstep scenes.

[[src-wiley]]
Wiley, “Eskimo,” _Eskimo_ EP (Wiley Kat Records, 2002):: Listening reference for sparse early grime rhythm and timbre. Library grids that cite it are independent studies, not transcriptions.

[[src-rp-boo]]
Library of Congress, link:https://www.loc.gov/resource/afc2022012.afc2022012_016_ms001/[“Follow The Music Interview: RP Boo”] (recorded 2023; accessed 2026-09-05):: Oral-history interview with Chicago DJ, dancer, and producer RP Boo. Used as context for footwork's dance, DJ, and producer community; no library grid transcribes his work.

== Experimental continuities

[[src-weightless]]
FACT, link:https://www.factmag.com/2014/10/01/mumdance-logos-different-circles-weightless/[“Mumdance and Logos launch label Different Circles”] (2014; accessed 2026-09-02):: Interview in which Logos and Mumdance describe weightless music as spacious, sometimes ambient or beatless club material that retains club-oriented production and engineering.

[[src-mumdance-archive]]
Mumdance, link:https://www.mumdance.com/interviews[interview archive] (accessed 2026-09-02):: Artist-maintained index documenting conversations with Logos and others about grime, weightless music, production, and related practices. Used as a route to first-person context, not as a source for transcription.

[[src-kode9]]
Chal Ravens, link:https://daily.redbullmusicacademy.com/2017/11/kode9-top-flight-interview[“Kode9 on Diggin' in the Carts and the Evolution of Hyperdub”], Red Bull Music Academy Daily (2017; accessed 2026-09-02):: Interview discussing the traces of jungle, garage, dub, reggae, dancehall, grime, and other forms within later electronic music.

[[src-principe]]
Príncipe Discos, link:https://principediscos.bandcamp.com/[label catalogue], Bandcamp (accessed 2026-09-02):: Primary listening catalogue for contemporary Lisbon dance music. Used only as broad listening context for the original batida-informed hybrid, not as the source of a transcription.

[[src-principe-profile]]
Ian McQuaid, link:https://daily.bandcamp.com/label-profile/principe-records-feature[“Príncipe and the New Sound of Lisbon”], Bandcamp Daily (2016; accessed 2026-09-02):: Label profile quoting Príncipe co-founder José Moura on batida, tarraxo, and Afro-house, and documenting DJ Nervoso's stripped, percussion-led approach in Lisbon's Quinta do Mocho community.

[[src-lycox]]
Joseph Francis, link:https://djmag.com/features/recognise-092-dj-lycox[“Recognise: DJ Lycox”], DJ Mag (2025; accessed 2026-09-02):: Interview with the batida producer on learning to make kuduro, absorbing other electronic styles, and maintaining batida's community and history while developing new production methods.

== Citation rules

For recordings, include artist, title, release or label, and year. For web sources, include author, page title, publisher, publication date when available, URL, and access date. A pattern page must identify registered sources through `source-refs` and explain whether each is evidence for a transcription, a listening reference, or contextual research.
