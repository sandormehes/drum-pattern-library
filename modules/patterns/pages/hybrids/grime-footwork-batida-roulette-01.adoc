== Pattern

[drum-pattern]
----
KICK   | X--- --x- x--- -x-- | --x- x--x ---- x---
SNARE  | ---- X--- ---- X--- | ---- X--- --x- ----
RIM    | --x- ---- -x-- ---- | x--- ---- ---- -x--
HAT    | x-x- xx-x x-xx -x-x | xx-x -x-x xxx- x--x
PERC   | -x-g x--- --x- x--g | ---- -x-- x-g- --x-
----

== Character

This faster study treats the kick lane as a two-bar call-and-response rather than a
foundation that repeats every beat. A recognizable grime snare supplies the frame;
rim and percussion replies make the rhythm feel circular and physical without resolving
into a four-on-the-floor pattern.

== Rhythmic roles

Call:: The first bar spreads four kick attacks across the grid with room after each one.
Response:: The second bar clusters its early kicks, then makes a late single attack answer the phrase.
Frame:: The beat-two snare remains stable; the bar-two ghost snare adds friction without replacing it.
Colour:: Rim and percussion trade short, contrasting responses around the low-drum events.

== Groove setting

Swing:: Apply a light 54–56% swing to hats, rim, and percussion only.
Accent:: Treat the first kick in each bar as an arrival; all other kicks should be lighter.
Space:: Use a compact room or slap delay on the rim, but leave snare and kick essentially dry.

== Performance notes

* At 160 BPM, program the hats at low velocity before adding more events.
* Use pitched tom, woodblock, or synthetic conga sounds for the percussion lane.
* For an arrangement change, mute the rim in bar one rather than adding a fill.
